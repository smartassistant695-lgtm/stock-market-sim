Words to know

Call
The right, but not the duty, to buy a stock at the strike price, so it gains value when the stock goes up.
Put
The right, but not the duty, to sell a stock at the strike price, so it gains value when the stock goes down.
Strike price
The price at which the option lets you buy (call) or sell (put) the stock.
Premium
The price of the option, quoted per share, and one contract covers 100 shares.
Expiration
The day the option ends, after which it is either worth something or worth nothing.
Break-even
The stock price at expiration where you neither make nor lose money, because the option is worth exactly what you paid.
Volatility
How much a stock's price tends to swing, and more swing makes options cost more because big moves are more likely.

For learning only, not financial advice. Prices are estimates from the Black-Scholes formula (European options, no dividends). A put is never priced below what it is worth if used today. Real options have fees, bid and ask prices and other details not shown here.
